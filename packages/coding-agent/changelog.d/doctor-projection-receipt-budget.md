### Fixed

- `gjc doctor` no longer reports `projection.coverage: limit_exceeded` and an `inconclusive` verdict with exit 3 on a healthy machine. The projection probe emitted one check per discovered item — measured at 193 skills in an ordinary operator home — and each check costs roughly 300-400 bytes of digest id, target id, and evidence, so the serialized receipt crossed the 64 KiB probe transport budget. The transport then discarded the *entire* receipt and replaced it with a single blocked check carrying empty evidence, which silently dropped every real `load_error`, `invalid_config`, `rejected`, and `quarantined` finding the check exists to surface. A healthy item never carried anything its surface coverage count did not already state, and its digest id was never a repair target (plugin repairs resolve through the dedicated plugin collector), so only actionable items become item checks now: the same home reports `verdict: healthy`, exit 0, and 37 completed checks instead of 30 checks and an inconclusive verdict.
- The projection probe is also responsible for fitting inside the transport budget rather than letting the client drop the surface. Per-surface and aggregate coverage checks are always retained, and a surface with more genuine warnings and errors than the budget allows sheds item checks deterministically and reports the truncation on its own coverage check as `limit_exceeded` with emitted/omitted counts. The transport limit and the producer budget are now one shared constant instead of two independent literals.
- Read-only doctor diagnosis no longer leaks suppressed log records to stderr. The probe disables both logger transports so diagnosis cannot write `.gjc/logs/` product state, which left Winston with no transport at all; it then wrote its own `Attempt to write logs with no transports` warning — including scanned `SKILL.md` paths, their contents, and full stack traces — straight to the process stderr the receipt shares.
